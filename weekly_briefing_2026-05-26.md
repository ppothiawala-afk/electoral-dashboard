## Environment Narrative

The national environment is shifting toward Democrats. ([Sabato's Crystal Ball](https://centerforpolitics.org/crystalball/the-senate-the-race-for-the-majority-is-not-a-toss-up-but-the-races-that-will-decide-it-are/)) notes that while the overall battle for the Senate majority isn't itself a toss-up, several of the individual races that will decide it are — a meaningful framing for the cycle's narrative. The moves in GA, NC, and OH this week are the clearest expression of that shift: two seats that were pure coin-flips in March now lean Democratic. The generic ballot has been drifting left since January, with independents breaking sharply toward Democrats ([Silver Bulletin](https://www.natesilver.net/p/generic-ballot-average-2026-nate-silver-bulletin-congress-polls)).

## Candidate News

- **Texas Senate primary:** Ken Paxton (R) is in a competitive runoff against incumbent Sen. John Cornyn (R) scheduled for May 27. ([Cook Political Report](https://www.cookpolitical.com/ratings/senate-race-ratings)) has the general at Likely R pending the outcome, with the expectation that a Paxton win would move the race toward competitive.

- **Iowa Senate:** Sen. Joni Ernst (R) has announced she will not seek re-election. The open seat — with Rep. Ashley Hinson (R) as the likely GOP nominee and Josh Turek (D) emerging on the Democratic side — is currently rated Likely R but is expected to tighten. ([Cook Political Report](https://www.cookpolitical.com/ratings/senate-race-ratings)).

- **House retirements:** Retirement pace continues to run well above historical averages, with Republicans accounting for a disproportionate share of open seats in competitive districts. No new high-profile retirements confirmed this specific week.
